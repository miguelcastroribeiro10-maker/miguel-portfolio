Brunello Cucinelli: India Market Entry

A market-entry strategy bringing Brunello Cucinelli's radical exclusivity to Mumbai, India's largest concentration of ultra-wealth, anchored by a Diwali launch at Jio World Plaza.

Graded 30/30
Read the full case study
Why Mumbai, why Jio World Plaza
  • Jio World Plaza is the only address housing 66 luxury brands including Louis Vuitton's largest India store, Dior, Cartier and Gucci — the sole location where Brunello Cucinelli plausibly belongs.
  • Giorgio Armani opened its Mumbai flagship at the same mall in 2025, its second India store after Delhi, setting a direct precedent.
  • Launch timed to Diwali, India's peak luxury consumption moment.
The capsule & the campaign
  • A two-piece India-exclusive capsule made in Italy using Indian banana fibre, a sustainable material bridging Solomeo and Mumbai.
  • "Threads of Light": a week-long Diwali launch built around a press preview, a VIP opening, and a gratitude-lantern ritual.
  • ATL support across out-of-home at Jio World Plaza, a dedicated digital strand, and a full-page placement in Vogue India's December issue.
Target clientele & KPIs
  • Four tiers, from UHNWI dynasties to emerging affluent prospects.
  • Targets: 5 editorial features within 30 days of launch, 80% of VIP guests activated in CRM within a week, and a 90%+ sell-through on the exclusive Diwali capsule.

Team project with Leonor Braga Fernandes, Amir Isse Sagal, Claudia La Cara, Marina Pellizzari and Vittoria Schiavoni.

HARIBO: Twin Snakes Go-To-Market

A full launch strategy for Twin Snakes, a two-sided sweet-and-sour gummy built to open the fast-growing sour segment for Haribo in Italy, without competing against its own range.

Graded 30/30
Read the full case study
Channel strategy
  • Priority 1, hypermarkets (Esselunga, Ipercoop, Carrefour, 902 stores): control of the promo aisle and checkout for maximum impulse visibility.
  • Priority 2, e-commerce (Amazon Italia, Esselungaacasa.it): the fastest-growing channel, allowing premium pricing without resistance.
  • Deliberately excluded: discount chains, where a price-only environment conflicts with the brand's perceived value.
A three-phase campaign
  • Launch: in-store promoters at checkout, letting shoppers bite first and discover the second flavour after. Entry price held at €1.49.
  • Summer: a Gardaland Water Park partnership with two snake-shaped water slides, turning the product into a physical attraction.
  • Halloween: five simultaneous touchpoints across Milan under #TrickOrTaste, including a branded tram and cinema activations.
Projected KPIs
  • 15% trial rate in activated stores at launch; 50K+ Gardaland visitors exposed over the summer.
  • €485,000 total activation budget, projecting €1.8M in incremental year-one revenue at a 3.7x expected ROI.

Team project with Leonor Braga Fernandes, Donika Hyseni and Hannah Charlotte Kahn.

Connecta: Mercado do Bairro

A three-month launch strategy for a hyperlocal marketplace connecting neighbourhood sellers, buyers and couriers — built to win on trust and locality rather than speed.

Presented directly to the founders
Read the full case study
Five strategic directions
  • The Local Countdown: sellers post what only exists today, turning every product into a deadline rather than a listing.
  • Neighbourhood Ambassadors: 3–5 local micro-creators become the visible face of the launch, paid on performance.
  • Anti-Giant Movement: positioning Connecta as a stance — your neighbourhood doesn't need Amazon.
Seller incentives
  • A first-sales guarantee: Connecta buys back unsold stock at sale price for the first 15 days.
  • Zero commission for the first 30 days, plus a free professional photoshoot for early sellers.

Closed with four open decisions left deliberately for the founders: entry category, launch neighbourhood, budget scale, and comfort with the positioning itself.

Original research · originally published on LinkedIn

The Desire-Accessibility Paradox: how luxury demand is splitting in two.

How K-shaped growth is splitting the luxury market, and what six houses reveal about which side they're building for.

Luxury demand today is polarising. Growth is concentrating among high-value clients, while many aspirational consumers are being priced out of the category altogether — a K-shaped split rather than a rising tide.

The underlying question every house has to answer is whether it is building visible cultural desire or deeper client relationships, rather than trying to optimise for both at once. High prices, on their own, aren't what creates exclusivity — disciplined pricing, scarcity and trusted relationships do more of that work than sticker shock ever could.

Key takeaways
  • The luxury market is increasingly polarised, with growth concentrated among high-value clients while many aspirational consumers are priced out.
  • Brands need to choose whether they're building visible cultural desire or deeper client relationships, rather than optimising for both at once.
  • High prices aren't the key driver of exclusivity — disciplined pricing, scarcity and trusted relationships build stronger long-term demand.

Observed, not assigned

Valentino

Going all in

Immersive spaces, bold aesthetics, generous gifting. A brand actively building cultural relevance.

Moncler

Impossible to ignore

A giant inflatable octopus on a rooftop: disruptive, designed to be shared. Audience expansion without losing cool.

Gucci

Reconnecting with identity

Told its origin story through monumental tapestries, a brand returning to itself after years of creative reinvention.

Audemars Piguet

Scarcity by design

A small window installation of cogs and gears. You only earned the gift if you solved their challenge first.

Hermès

The power of silence

No activation, no gifting, just product, displayed in silence. The brand doesn't come to you, you come to it.
